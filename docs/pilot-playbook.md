# First contribution pilot

Memory Box is open to everyone. Waterloo is the first neighborhood, with fictional starters clearly separated from contributions. The pilot should establish whether a newcomer understands the world, finds something worth reacting to and can leave a real piece of it. Working QA and an indexed corpus do not establish that people want to contribute or return.

## Run two different sessions

**Uncoached arrival:** invite five to ten people individually, including mobile users and people outside Waterloo. Send the clean homepage, https://memory-box-commons.vercel.app/ . Give no tour, preferred arrival, target corner or contribution instructions. Ask them to use it for five minutes and think aloud if comfortable. Stay quiet until they finish or request help. Any help changes that observation from uncoached to assisted; record the distinction. Genuine eligible first visits may enroll in the existing arrival experiment. Do not create fake visitors, reset their storage or ask them to return just to improve a metric.

**Small collaborative session:** separately invite a few people to one unfinished piece through its Meet here link. Ask them to make something together: finish a creature, add a wrong turn to the maze, extend a joke or leave a response beside an original. Observe whether the invitation and in-place build flow explain themselves. Shared links and `?arrival=` previews bypass arrival enrollment, so this is collaboration/usability evidence rather than an arrival A/B comparison.

Start with a small group and a specific unfinished thing. A busy scheduled session tests a different experience from discovering a quiet public world alone. Keep both observations; do not combine their conversion percentages.

## Invitation drafts

Uncoached, unsent:

> I’m testing Memory Box, a shared canvas of pixels, notes, images and people’s odd little stories. Could you open https://memory-box-commons.vercel.app/ and spend five minutes with it? I’m interested in what you naturally do and what is confusing. You don’t have to post anything. Afterward, tell me what you thought it was and what you wanted to do next.

Collaborative, unsent:

> Want to help make something ridiculous in Memory Box for ten minutes? Here’s the unfinished piece: [paste its Meet here link]. Add to it however you like: pixels, an image, a note or a response. Tell me where the app gets in your way.

These are templates for the user to review and send. No outreach, participant recruitment or public contribution is performed by this runbook.

## What to observe

Keep aggregate counts, device classes and broad failure categories. Do not store identities, authored text, photos, search queries or recordings unless a separate consent process calls for them.

| Moment | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| First 30 seconds | Can they describe the world? Do they notice a real original rather than only controls? |
| Exploration | Do they pan or zoom, open an original and find a different area without instructions? |
| Intent | Do they spontaneously choose to paint, leave a note/photo or build on an original? |
| Accepted contribution | Does the server confirm it, and can they locate it afterward? A preview is not a saved contribution. |
| Interruption | In an explicitly labelled recovery task, can they dismiss/reload and recover words or pixels? Do not mix this coached task with natural arrival behavior. |
| Collaboration | Can another person see the accepted change and respond beside it without a facilitator? |
| Return | Do they independently revisit a place or a response later? Separate an invited test revisit from a spontaneous return. |

Ask afterward: “What did you think this was?”, “What would you leave if you used it again?”, “What made you hesitate?” and “Would you come back, and what would you expect to find?” A positive interview answer is not observed retention.

## Prepare the world and close the loop

Use real, clearly authored unfinished builds with a small obvious next move and room nearby. Avoid completing every picture before people arrive. Review and publish invitations yourself; do not fill the world with generated activity, invented participants or unlabelled examples. Keep the rest of the map open for unrelated, chaotic contributions.

Before recruitment, verify an accepted note, image and paint stroke on desktop/mobile, interruption recovery, a second independent session receiving the change and the ability to find/open the saved original. Resolve content-loss, blocked mobile gestures and misleading save feedback before inviting a larger group.

After the first five to ten observations, pick the most common failed transition and fix it. Repeat with fresh people. The small pilot produces concrete usability findings, not statistical proof of adoption or an arrival winner. Leave the running experiment’s allocation, metrics and matured conversion/return windows intact; see [arrival experiment operations](arrival-experiment.md).
